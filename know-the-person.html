Know the Person

This page was intentionally left off the homepage. This site is about the quality of his research — which stands on its own, without context or explanation. What follows is the cherry on top of a first-class cake.

Hekmat Antaki — smiling, loving father. Those who knew him say he rarely got angry. He answered every letter he received from scientists around the world, without exception. He loved chemistry.

Orphaned at five. He belonged to Egypt's Greek Orthodox minority. He lost his wife (Violette) to cancer in 1971 and raised three young children (Robert, Charles, André) alone — never showing his grief.

He lived through the wars of 1956, 1962, and 1967. He worked through the social and political upheaval that followed Suez, through the dismantling of Egypt's independent professional class, through the isolation of Egyptian science from Western networks. The chemistry spoke. The name was in the shadow.

He retired on a government pension. He never made money from his work. He never sought recognition.

"Not in science — they will realise it."
